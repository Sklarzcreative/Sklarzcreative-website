Trust-First Content Scorecard

Twenty statements. Five trust signals. One clearer decision about what to change next.

Signals
Five

Twenty
Total

How to use it

One touchpoint. Twenty decisions.

Choose one homepage, landing page, social profile, campaign, email, sales page, or piece of content. Score what a first-time reader would actually find there. Complete all twenty statements for a reliable total.

  • 0 = missing
  • 1 = partial or inconsistent
  • 2 = clear and present

Clarity

Can people quickly understand what this is and why it matters?

0/8
1. A first-time visitor can tell what the brand or offer does.
2. The intended audience is recognizable from the language and context.
3. The main point is easy to identify without decoding jargon.
4. The next useful action is clear.

Consistency

Do the pieces agree with one another?

0/8
1. The message matches what the brand says on its other important channels.
2. Tone and terminology are recognizable rather than randomly changing.
3. Claims and promises are consistent across the customer journey.
4. The visual treatment supports the same positioning as the words.

Credibility

Is there enough reason to believe the message?

0/8
1. Important claims are supported by evidence, examples, context, or specificity.
2. The content distinguishes fact, interpretation, opinion, and aspiration when needed.
3. Limitations or uncertainty are acknowledged when they materially affect the claim.
4. The brand avoids exaggeration that would require the audience to take too much on faith.

Connection

Does the communication understand the person on the other side?

0/8
1. The content reflects a real audience problem, question, motivation, or decision.
2. The language respects the audience rather than talking down to them or manufacturing fear.
3. The content provides useful value before demanding attention, trust, or action.
4. The tone feels human and appropriate to the relationship the brand is trying to build.

Conversion

Does the content help the right person move forward?

0/8
1. The primary CTA fits the content and the audience stage.
2. The destination delivers what the CTA appears to promise.
3. The next step is easy enough to complete without unnecessary friction.
4. The content has one primary job instead of asking for several unrelated actions.
0/40

Turn the score into a decision

Do not fix everything at once.

Start with the lowest-scoring signal that matters most to the customer decision you are trying to support. One change, made properly, moves a score further than five made carelessly.

  • 32–40 Strong trust system
  • 24–31 Solid foundation
  • 16–23 Inconsistent signals
  • 0–15 Rebuild the basics
1. Lowest signal
2. One change to make in the next 7 days
3. One piece of proof or context to add
4. One destination or CTA to fix

Recheck before publishing: What is the point? Who is it for? What should they think or do afterward? Where does it lead next? What is the one primary job?

Four of the five signals scored here — clarity, consistency, credibility, and connection — come from the trust lens introduced in File 001 of The Trust Files. Conversion is added because a score is only useful if it changes what happens next.
