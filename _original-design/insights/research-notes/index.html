Evidence & Context

Research Notes

Short, source-conscious examinations of useful evidence, emerging ideas, and claims that deserve a closer look. The goal is to distinguish what is known, what is suggested, and what remains uncertain.

Editorial Standard

Evidence before certainty.

Research Notes are not designed to make weak evidence sound exciting. Scientific, legal, market, and behavioral claims should be separated from interpretation and opinion. Where evidence is early, mixed, indirect, or incomplete, the language should say so.

1 · QuestionStart with one clear claim or problem worth examining.
2 · EvidencePrioritize primary, authoritative, and directly relevant sources.
3 · LimitsIdentify uncertainty, missing context, and competing explanations.
4 · ImplicationExplain what the evidence reasonably changes for a brand, founder, or audience.

Research Integrity

“Interesting” is not the same as “established.”

Research Notes are designed to preserve that distinction. When a finding is preliminary, narrow, conflicting, or based on indirect evidence, the communication should make those limits visible rather than hide them behind confident language.
