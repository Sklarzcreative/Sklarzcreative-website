## LinkedIn newsletter launch post

> Primary job: Awareness · CTA: Read + subscribe

I've been looking for a place to go deeper than a social post allows.

So I'm starting Curves Ahead.

It's a publication about the shifts I'm seeing across business, marketing,
technology, media and emerging industries, particularly the ones that may look
small now but could matter much more later.

The first edition starts with a question I keep coming back to:

As AI makes execution dramatically easier, what actually becomes more valuable?

My answer is judgment.

Producing something is getting cheaper. Deciding what deserves to be produced,
what matters, what to ignore and what should happen next is not.

That distinction has implications far beyond AI.

Issue 01: AI Is Making Execution Cheap. Judgment Is Becoming More Valuable.

If those are the kinds of questions you like thinking about, I'd be glad to have
you reading along.

Curves Ahead is a publication by Cassandra Sklarz about emerging ideas in

## Community / Reddit version

> Primary job: Community

I'm increasingly convinced that one of the biggest effects of AI on business
won't be cheaper writing or design.

It'll be that competent execution becomes abundant.

A business can already generate dozens of reasonable concepts, posts, designs or
summaries quickly.

But that doesn't answer:

Which idea is actually worth pursuing?
Which information is credible?
What should the company be known for?
Which process should be automated?
Which process shouldn't exist in the first place?

In some ways AI makes weak strategy more dangerous because unclear thinking can
now become polished output extremely quickly.

My working theory is that the scarce resource is moving upstream from production
toward judgment.

For people using AI in real business workflows: where has it genuinely removed
work, and where has it just moved the bottleneck somewhere else?

### Edition 02 Substack Note teaser

Growth rarely weakens a brand all at once. More often, it happens through a
series of reasonable decisions. A new service. Another channel. More automation.
A bigger team. Individually, each can make sense. Together, they can leave a
company bigger and harder to recognize. The question isn't only what can scale.
It's what is valuable enough to protect.
