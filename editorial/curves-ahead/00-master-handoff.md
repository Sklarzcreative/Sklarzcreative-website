## Working publication brand

- **Name:** CURVES AHEAD
- **Tagline:** Emerging ideas in business, marketing, technology and media.
- **Byline:** By Cassandra Sklarz
- **Expanded editorial promise:** Curves Ahead examines the shifts shaping
  business, brands, technology, media, science and emerging industries, with a
  focus on what matters, why it matters and what may come next.

## Why this name

Cassandra originally liked "Ahead of the Curve," but it is heavily used. Curves
Ahead preserves the forward-looking meaning while adding a subtle personal
double meaning. The body-related wordplay should remain understated and never
dominate the publication's professional identity.

## Naming shortlist / status

1. **Curves Ahead** — working winner; continue due diligence before
   legal/trademark commitment.
2. **Curve Theory** — creatively strong but retired as a primary brand because
   an existing design/branding business uses Curve Theory / curvetheory.com.
3. **Curve Appeal** — memorable but crowded and carries stronger
   fashion/body/beauty associations; not recommended as flagship.
4. **Signals Ahead** — strong fallback if Curves Ahead later presents a
   clearance problem.

## Publication positioning

Curves Ahead is not a generic marketing newsletter and should not read like a
corporate Sklarz Creative bulletin. It is a personal/editorial publication led
by Cassandra's judgment, research, observations, analysis and point of view.

**Primary audience:**

- Founders and entrepreneurs
- Marketing and brand leaders
- Strategists and consultants
- Creators and media professionals
- Technology-curious business leaders
- Science / innovation communicators
- Emerging-industry professionals
- Technically minded founders who need to understand change without following
  every trend

**Primary publication job: AUTHORITY.**
Derivatives may have a different single primary job such as Awareness,
Engagement, Traffic, Community or Lead Generation.

## Sklarz Creative content gate

Every derivative should answer:

1. What is the point?
2. Who is it for?
3. What outcome should it create?
4. What happens next?

Traffic, Lead Generation or Community.

- Do not mention AI-assisted writing in reader-facing publication copy unless AI
  is substantively relevant to the subject.

## AI / writing position

AI is production infrastructure, not the reader-facing brand. Curves Ahead
should present Cassandra's ideas, judgment, research, analysis and perspective.
Never invent personal experience, research, client stories or opinions.
Factual/scientific/current claims should be verified and responsibly framed.

## First six editions

**01 · AI Is Making Execution Cheap. Judgment Is Becoming More Valuable.**
Department: The Breakdown / Ahead of the Curve · Primary job: Authority
Core thesis: AI is commoditizing competent execution. Durable advantage moves
toward judgment, proprietary knowledge, originality, trust, taste and deciding
what deserves to be produced at all.

**02 · Growth Without Losing the Plot: What Brands Should Protect as They
Scale.**
Department: Behind the Brand · Primary job: Authority
Status: A substantial ready-to-publish article and repurposing pack already
exist in Drive. Migrate, do not rewrite from scratch.

**03 · Why Newsletters Are Becoming Interesting Again.**
Department: The Breakdown · Primary job: Authority
Core thesis: Newsletters are interesting again not because email is new, but
because social distribution became fragmented, short-form became abundant,
direct relationships became more valuable, and newsletters create continuity and
an owned audience layer.

## Edition 01 core launch framing

Do not say "I started a newsletter about AI." Use the broader publication
framing:

> "I've been looking for a place to go deeper than a social post allows. So I'm
> starting Curves Ahead. It's a publication about the shifts I'm seeing across
> business, marketing, technology, media and emerging industries, particularly
> the ones that may look small now but could matter much more later."

Then introduce the first issue's question:

> "As AI makes execution dramatically easier, what actually becomes more
> valuable? My answer is judgment."
