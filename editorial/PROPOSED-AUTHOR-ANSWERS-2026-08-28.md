## Q1 · Edition 01 — a concrete example

**What the piece needs.** Authority scored 6 before the edit and 6 after,
because the article contains no concrete instance anywhere. An editor cannot
supply one without fabricating it. The gap is flagged in the edited text at the
automation section, immediately after *"The ability to automate something is not
evidence that it should exist."*

**Proposed answer, in Cassandra's words as given:**

> I set up an automated tracker for Curves Ahead because I wanted the publishing
> work and progress centralized. Instead of replacing a clear workflow, it
> became another system to monitor while higher-priority client work needed
> attention. I turned it off until it had a defined job and a real workflow to
> replace.

**Why it fits.** It is the article's own argument demonstrated on the author
rather than asserted about businesses in general — a tool added because it
*could* perform a task, not because a job needed doing. It also carries the
piece's strongest line without repeating it.

**What it would change.** This is the single largest available gain in
Edition 01. It moves the piece from argued to demonstrated and is the only thing
that lifts Authority off its ceiling.

**Check before approving:** is this account accurate as written, and is there
anything in it — a tool name, a client reference, a date — that should not be
public?

## Q2 · Edition 01 — the unanswered objection

**What the piece needs.** A sceptical reader will already have thought it, and
the article never engages it, which makes it easier to dismiss than it deserves.
The objection as a reader would put it: *these systems are getting better at
judgment-shaped work too — evaluating, ranking, critiquing, choosing between
options — and "judgment is the durable advantage" is a very convenient thing for
a strategist to believe about the future of strategists.*

**Proposed answer, in Cassandra's words as given:**

> The objection is partly right. AI will increasingly perform work that looks
> like judgment, including ranking, critiquing, and choosing among options. The
> durable human advantage is not making every choice manually. It is defining
> the goal, deciding which tradeoffs matter, evaluating context the system may
> not hold, and remaining accountable for the consequences.

**Why it fits.** It concedes the strong form of the objection rather than
deflecting it, then narrows the thesis to something defensible — goal-setting,
trade-off weighting, context the model does not hold, and accountability. That
is a smaller claim than the article currently implies, and a more durable one.

**Editorial note.** Accepting this means the piece's thesis narrows. That is an
improvement, not a retreat, but the surrounding paragraphs would be adjusted to
match so the article does not claim more elsewhere than it concedes here.

## Q3 · Edition 03 — a concrete observation

**What the piece needs.** Same gap as Edition 01. Best placement is the
community-loop section or *"newsletters compound."*

**Proposed answer, in Cassandra's words as given:**

> Testing across multiple social platforms showed me that visibility and
> relationship are not the same thing. A post can receive discovery or
> engagement and then disappear without creating an ongoing connection. A
> newsletter gives the ideas a continuing home and gives readers a deliberate
> way to return.

**Why it fits.** It is a first-hand observation from work actually done, and it
states the article's central distinction — discovery versus relationship — as
something seen rather than reasoned.

**Check before approving:** is "testing across multiple social platforms" the
accurate description of what happened? The standard forbids inflating a real
observation into a larger research claim, and *"I've been testing"* is only
usable when actual work supports it.

## Q4 · Edition 03 — which open question is genuinely held

**What the piece needs.** Two of the three original "what I'm watching" items
restated sections the reader had just finished, so they were cut. The section is
now thin and should be genuinely forward-looking rather than padded back out.

**Proposed answer, in Cassandra's words as given — she takes both:**

> Both questions matter. Inbox attention can become crowded, and an email list
> is portable rather than completely owned because deliverability and filtering
> are still mediated. Newsletters remain valuable because the relationship is
> more direct, but only when the content earns attention rather than treating
> the inbox as guaranteed reach.

**Why it fits.** It holds both open questions instead of picking one, and lands
on a qualification that is genuinely hers: the newsletter advantage is
conditional on earning attention, not automatic. That final clause is the
strongest sentence in the answer and is the one worth carrying into the piece.

**Editorial note.** This concedes that "owned audience" is overstated — the
article currently leans on it. As with Q2, the surrounding argument would be
adjusted so the piece does not claim more elsewhere than it concedes here.
