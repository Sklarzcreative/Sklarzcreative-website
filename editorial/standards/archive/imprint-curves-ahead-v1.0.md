## The promise

> **Emerging ideas in business, marketing, technology and media.**

Everything below serves that sentence. A piece that does not deliver on it is
not a Curves Ahead piece, whatever else is good about it.

## Default mode

**Full Editorial Pass.** Curves Ahead does not take a Light Edit as its primary
pass; the publication gate in §13 of the standard assumes the full sequence has
run.

## The editorial lens

Five questions the lens tends to ask:

- What is changing?
- What actually matters?
- What are people missing?
- Why does it matter?
- What might happen next?

**These are diagnostic questions, not headings.** Do not force an article into
them as a structure. If a draft arrives already shaped as five sections
answering these in order, that is a formulaic-structure finding under §5, not a
success.

The third question — *what are people missing* — is usually where a Curves Ahead
piece earns its existence. An article that only answers the first two is a news
summary. The reader can get that anywhere.

## What "emerging" obliges

Writing about things that are still in motion sets a specific evidential
standard, and §4.12 of the standard applies with unusual force:

- Early-stage evidence must be **labelled as early-stage in the prose**, not
  only in a footnote.
- A prediction must read as a prediction. "This will reshape X" is a forecast
  wearing a fact's clothes.
- Where the honest answer is that nobody yet knows, say that. It is more
  interesting than a manufactured conclusion, and it ages far better.

An emerging-ideas publication that overclaims once is harder to trust
afterwards on everything else it publishes.

## Structure

Per §5's anti-template rule, vary the architecture. For this imprint the useful
forms are usually:

essay · analysis · field note · critique · trend analysis · research
interpretation · question-driven exploration

Check the two most recently published Curves Ahead pieces before settling the
structure of a third. Same shape three times running is a finding.

## The gate

The eleven-point publication gate in §13 of the standard applies in full. No
Curves Ahead piece is marked `READY TO PUBLISH` with any point failing.

The workflow is fixed:

```
draft
  → Editorial Director · Full Editorial Pass
    → Cassandra's review and approval
      → Editorial Director · Final Proof
        → publish
```

## One thing the publication never does

Curves Ahead covers technology, which means it will regularly cover AI. The
publication writes *about* the subject; it never advertises how its own pages
were produced, and no published Curves Ahead page mentions this agent, this
workflow, or the use of AI in producing it. That is a matter of editorial
convention — a reader is owed a well-made argument and honest sourcing, not a
tour of the newsroom.

The corollary is a hard constraint on the agent, not a licence: nothing produced
under this profile may claim work, experience or research that did not happen.
See §4.13 and §6.
