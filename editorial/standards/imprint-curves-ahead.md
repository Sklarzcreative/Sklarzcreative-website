## The promise

> **Emerging ideas in business, marketing, technology and media.**

Expanded: Curves Ahead examines the shifts shaping business, brands, technology,
media, science and emerging industries, with a focus on what matters, why it
matters and what may come next.

Byline: **By Cassandra Sklarz.** Primary publication job: **Authority.**

## What this publication is not

**Curves Ahead is not a corporate Sklarz Creative bulletin.** It is a personal
editorial publication led by Cassandra's judgment, research, observations,
analysis and point of view.

That distinction has a direct editorial consequence: first person is native
here, not a lapse to be edited out. A draft that reads like a consultancy
speaking rather than a person thinking has drifted, and the fix is to restore
the person, not to smooth the prose further.

The house CTA is *subscribe*, not *hire us*. Service-led conversion belongs on
the website archive version; inside the publication it flattens the voice into
marketing.

## Editorial pillars

Strategy & Growth · Technology & Innovation · Brands & Marketing · Media &
Culture · Science & Emerging Industries · Field Notes (founder-operator
observations)

The publication is deliberately broader than AI, marketing, or any single
industry. A run of editions clustered in one pillar is worth flagging.

## Departments

| Department | Covers |
| --- | --- |
| **Ahead of the Curve** | Forward-looking shifts and emerging signals |
| **The Breakdown** | Deeper strategic analysis |
| **Field Notes** | First-person observations from actual work, research and experiments |
| **Worth Watching** | Short developments deserving attention |
| **Research Notes** | Science, evidence, data and emerging industries |
| **Behind the Brand** | Positioning, marketing, growth and business-building decisions |

**Field Notes carries a special obligation.** The department's promise is
observation from actual work. §4.13 of the standard is therefore not merely
active but load-bearing: a Field Note built on an experience that did not happen
is a fabricated dateline, not a stylistic problem. If the material is not there,
the piece belongs in another department.

## The editorial lens

- What is changing?
- What actually matters?
- What are people missing?
- Why does it matter?
- What might happen next?

**Diagnostic questions, not headings.** A draft already shaped as five sections
answering these in order is a formulaic-structure finding under §5, not a
success.

The third — *what are people missing* — is usually where a Curves Ahead piece
earns its existence. An article that only answers the first two is a news
summary, and the reader can get that anywhere.

## The editorial template

The handoff supplies a seven-step thinking framework: Opening Signal · Context ·
What People Are Missing · Why It Matters · Breakdown / evidence / examples ·
What I'm Watching · Takeaway.

It is explicitly **not to be forced into every article as headings.** Treat it
as a checklist for whether the argument is complete, then let the structure
follow the argument, per §5's anti-template rule. The useful forms here are
essay, analysis, field note, critique, trend analysis, research interpretation,
and question-driven exploration.

Check the two most recently published editions before settling the structure of
a third. The same shape three times running is a finding.

## The content gate

Every piece and every derivative should answer:

1. What is the point?
2. Who is it for?
3. What outcome should it create?
4. What happens next?

And carry exactly **one primary job**: Awareness, Authority, Engagement,
Traffic, Lead Generation, or Community. A flagship edition's job is Authority.
A derivative doing two jobs is doing neither.

## What "emerging" obliges

Writing about things still in motion sets a specific evidential standard, and
§4.12 applies with unusual force:

- Early-stage evidence must be **labelled as early-stage in the prose**, not
  only in a footnote.
- A prediction must read as a prediction. "This will reshape X" is a forecast
  wearing a fact's clothes.
- Where the honest answer is that nobody yet knows, say that. It is more
  interesting than a manufactured conclusion, and it ages far better.
- **Platform mechanics and market figures decay.** Any claim about how LinkedIn,
  Substack, or any platform currently works needs a live check against official
  documentation before publication, and the argument should stand even when the
  figures move. Where a draft carries such a claim without a source, flag it —
  do not verify it from memory.

An emerging-ideas publication that overclaims once is harder to trust afterwards
on everything else it publishes.

## The AI rule, stated precisely

From the handoff: **AI is production infrastructure, not the reader-facing
brand.** Reader-facing publication copy does not mention AI-assisted writing
*unless AI is substantively relevant to the subject*.

Two things that are easy to conflate:

- **AI as subject** is entirely in scope, and is the subject of Edition 01 and
  much of the backlog. Analysing the shift is the publication doing its job.
- **AI as production method** stays out of reader-facing copy. Not concealment —
  editorial convention. A reader is owed a well-made argument and honest
  sourcing, not a tour of the newsroom.

The corollary is a hard constraint on this agent, never a licence: nothing
produced under this profile may claim work, experience or research that did not
happen. See §4.13 and §6.

## Never fabricate a premise

From the backlog document, and specific enough to quote:

> Do not fabricate premises such as "I spent the week investigating this" unless
> Cassandra actually did. Use true language such as "I've been looking into…,"
> "Something I've been noticing…," "I keep seeing…," "I've been testing…," or
> "After comparing…" **only when supported by actual work.**

This is the most likely place an editor makes things worse. A draft that says "I
keep seeing" is making a modest, true claim; "sharpening" it into "in my work
with clients I have repeatedly found" invents a body of evidence. That edit
reads better and is a fabrication.

## Voice reminders from the handoff

Curves Ahead should sound **human, smart, curious, analytical, specific and
occasionally witty.** Read alongside
[`voice-cassandra-sklarz.md`](./voice-cassandra-sklarz.md).

The name itself carries an understated personal double meaning. It should stay
understated and never dominate the publication's professional identity — which
means the editor does not lean on it, extend it into curve metaphors, or nudge
it toward a joke.

## The gate

The eleven-point publication gate in §13 of the standard applies in full. No
Curves Ahead piece is marked `READY TO PUBLISH` with any point failing.

```
draft
  → Editorial Director · Full Editorial Pass
    → Cassandra's review and approval
      → Editorial Director · Final Proof
        → publish
```

Publication order after the proof, per the handoff: LinkedIn Newsletter +
Substack **the same day** — exclusivity is not manufactured while the audience
is small — then the sklarzcreative.com archive, then platform derivatives, then
community discussion, with feedback returning to the backlog.

This agent's responsibility ends at the proof. It does not publish, does not
write to `insights/`, and does not produce derivatives unless asked.
