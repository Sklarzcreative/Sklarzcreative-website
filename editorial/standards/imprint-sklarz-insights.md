## The promise

From the Articles hub:

> Longer pieces on brand clarity, trust, storytelling, and the judgement calls
> behind marketing decisions — **written to help a reader decide something
> rather than to add to the pile.**

And the stated editorial standard:

> **Useful first. Defensible second. Interesting third.**
>
> Claims should be proportionate to the evidence, uncertainty should be visible,
> and practical implications should be clear.

That is the gate this imprint already publishes against. The Editorial Director
enforces it rather than replacing it.

## Default mode

**Full Editorial Pass** for a new article. **Light Edit** for a revision to a
published page. **Final Proof** before anything goes live.

## The four formats

| Format | Path | What the lens asks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The Trust Files** | `insights/the-trust-files/` | Does this hold as an investigation — evidence, contradiction, consequence? |
| **Articles** | `insights/articles/` | Can the reader decide something they could not decide before? |
| **Research Notes** | `insights/research-notes/` | Are the evidence, its limits, and its implications separately stated? |
| **Resources** | `insights/resources/` | Is the tool usable without the article that explains it? |

Research Notes carry the heaviest evidential burden: the format's own promise is
"evidence, limits, implications," so §6 of the standard is not satisfied unless
all three are visibly present.

## Site constraints that are also editorial constraints

Two of the repository's load-bearing rules bind the copy, not just the code:

1. **Never assert anything untrue** — no invented metrics, client logos,
   testimonials, or publication dates.
2. **A page must not imply that something exists when it does not.** The Trust
   Files page states that File 001's audio is still in production, because it
   is. Aspirational phrasing that reads as a claim is an editorial error here,
   not a marketing choice.

## Publishing

The agent never writes to `insights/`. A cleared piece is moved into the site by
hand, as a normal commit, by a human who has read it. See §12 of the standard.
