## Mode note — why not a Full Editorial Pass

Full Editorial Pass is the Curves Ahead default, and I did not use it here.

The master handoff says this article and its pack "are source material.
**Migrate, do not rewrite from scratch.** Preserve the argument and strongest
original language." The piece has also already been through a full editorial
pass in its previous life and was marked ready to publish. Running a second full
pass over approved language is the exact failure the standard's Final Proof rules
exist to prevent — it invites an editor to relitigate settled decisions because
the mode permits it.

So: structure preserved entirely, all seven sections intact in order, no
resequencing, no merging. Changes are the migration items the handoff explicitly
authorises, plus repetition and wording. Everything structural is flagged, not
acted on.

## A · Editorial verdict

### `READY AFTER MINOR EDITS`

The highest verdict of the three editions, and deserved. This is the strongest
draft in the launch trilogy on every measure except one, and that one is
significant enough to need your decision before it publishes.

The prose is genuinely good: real paragraphs, varied sentence length, concrete

read as generated, because it wasn't shaped by the habit that shapes them.

What holds it at *minor edits* rather than *ready*: it does not currently sound
like Curves Ahead.

## C · Key editorial notes

**1 · Zero instances of "I." This is the finding.**
Edition 01 has seven. Edition 03 has several. This piece has none, and its
publishing pack records *"removed unnecessary first-person hedging such as 'I
think'"* as a completed improvement.

That was the right call for a Sklarz Creative company article. It is the wrong
call for Curves Ahead, whose profile states the publication is led by your
judgment and is explicitly **not** a corporate bulletin. Read alongside the other
two editions, this one reads as the consultancy speaking rather than you
thinking.

**I have not restored it.** Reversing a deliberate editorial decision of yours
without asking is precisely what the preservation rule forbids, and "the other
editions do it differently" is not authority to overrule you. But this is the
largest gap between Edition 02 and the rest of the publication, and it should be
decided rather than inherited. Flagged in the text with the three natural
locations.

**2 · The voice profile and the old pack disagree, and one has to give.**
Related but distinct. The pack treats hedging as weakness to remove; the voice
profile treats calibrated hedging as a feature of your argument, and lists
"comfortable with nuance" and "confident enough to say I don't think that
matters" as core traits.

Both cannot be house style. This is logged as an open item in the standards
changelog rather than resolved by me, because it is a standing decision about how
you write, not a call about this one piece.

**3 · The migration items the handoff authorises, applied.**
Masthead, edition number, department and "By Cassandra Sklarz" added. The Sklarz
Creative services CTA and author bio replaced with the subscribe CTA the launch
pack specifies verbatim for the newsletter version. Both originals are preserved
in the draft and belong on the website archive version, where the handoff says to
keep the stronger conversion.

**4 · Heading case, now consistent with the publication.**
The draft used Title Case ("Protect the Core Promise"); Editions 01 and 03 use
sentence case. Normalised to sentence case, and the numbered sections now use
the middot form the Curves Ahead documents use elsewhere. Trivial on its own,
visible when three editions sit in one archive.

**5 · One genuine fragment stack, compressed.**
*"More services require more explanations. More audiences create more messaging.
More products create more navigation. More teams create more terminology. More
channels create more campaigns competing for attention."* — five parallel
sentences, the one place this piece does what the other two do constantly.
Rejoined into a single sentence. The "more products / more navigation" clause was
dropped in the join as the weakest of the five; say so if you want it back.

**6 · Two small repetitions and two soft phrases.**
*"They are not the same thing"* appeared twice, and *"Those are not always the
same thing"* a third time in the same family — one folded inline, one kept. *"The
part of growth we do not talk about enough"* became *"the part of growth that
gets least attention"* — the original is a mild cliché that asserts something
about a conversation rather than about growth. *"In fact"* cut once. That is the
entire line-level intervention; the prose did not need more.

**7 · The template structure — flagged, deliberately not fixed.**
Intro → problem → seven numbered lessons → test → conclusion is the shape the
anti-template rule exists to interrupt. But it is not wrong here: this is a
checklist piece and a checklist earns numbered sections, and Light Edit preserves
structure regardless.

Worth tracking rather than changing. Editions 01 and 03 are both essays, so the
publication is at two-to-one, not in a rut. If Edition 04 arrives as another
numbered list, that is the moment to intervene.

**8 · What this edition proves about the other two.**
Not a note about this draft, but the most useful thing this pass surfaced: the
fragment-stack habit in Editions 01 and 03 is not how you write. It is how one
production process writes. Edition 02 came from a different process and does not
have it. That means the fix for the other two is not a stylistic constraint
imposed on your voice — it is closer to restoring it.

## E · Change summary

- **Applied the handoff's migration items**: Curves Ahead masthead, edition
  number, department, byline; newsletter subscribe CTA replacing the services CTA
  and author bio, both of which survive in the draft for the website version.
- **Normalised headings** to sentence case and the publication's numbering style.
- **Compressed one five-sentence parallel stack** into a single sentence.
- **Removed two repetitions** of "they are not the same thing" and one "in fact."
- **Replaced one soft phrase** ("the part of growth we do not talk about enough").
- **Flagged three things rather than acting**: the absent first person, the
  hedging contradiction, and the template structure.

### Not changed

Structure entirely — all seven sections, in order, unmerged. No argument altered,
softened or sharpened. No example, statistic or claim added. Roughly 96% of the
body text is byte-identical to the draft, which is what a Light Edit on a strong
piece should look like.
